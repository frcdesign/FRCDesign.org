The "intake" of an FRC robot is the mechanism used to collect gamepieces from the floor and transfer them to your other mechanisms. It is often the most important mechanism on your robot after the drivetrain. If the intake breaks or works poorly or takes a long time, in most cases, you can't even do the most basic actions with the gamepieces. It is also the way the driver interacts with the gamepieces, so it often ties into your on-field strategy and efficiency.

Examples gifs of intake in action on the field

The form an intake can take depends on the gamepiece and constraints of the game, but there are a couple of common and tested styles you can seek to replicate, such as over-the-bumper, under-the-bumper, claws, etc. How you deploy the intake is also an important decision with tradeoffs to make depending on your priorities and experience. The failure and iteration process for intakes has been done over the entirety of FRC, so don't be afraid to use existing designs as a starting point for your own team's iteration.

Examples of intake models

Ignoring the deployment for now (pivot design, generally the easiest deployment method, is covered in 2C), there are a number of things you can start thinking about to design a good intake. 

CARDS for golden rules


1. Build Robust intakes
2. Simplify the driving or autonomous skill (and time) required
3. Maximize grippiness
4. compression
5. Surface speed and torque
6. Use sensors


There's also some general rules you can follow to help you design a better intake, written out by Andrew Torrance from Team 254 ([link to the ChiefDelphi post](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/besiktas-rsports-9483-2023-off-season-build-blog/440340/15)).
